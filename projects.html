Thesis project 2025 · R&D project

Design & development of a high altitude long endurance UAV

A thorough study of existing HALE platforms revealed a clear gap. No small-scale, locally manufacturable HALE UAV had been developed for regional use. All current systems are large, costly, and designed for military or industrial-scale operations, leaving significant room for optimisation at a smaller scale.

This project set out to fill that gap by developing a compact HALE UAV tailored for local manufacturing and regional deployment, studying hybrid energy systems and energy-efficient flight strategies at small scale. The aircraft integrates autonomous thermal soaring, intelligent path planning, and high-efficiency batteries, with the airframe designed for future solar integration to extend endurance beyond battery limits.

The design was validated through CFD, FEA and FSI analysis using an MDO framework, then successfully prototyped and flight tested at altitudes up to 4,000 m.

Pasban H-VTOL

Pasban H-VTOL is a long-endurance hybrid VTOL UAV designed for persistent intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance operations. The platform combines vertical takeoff and landing capability with a fixed-wing cruise configuration, enabling rapid deployment from confined or unprepared locations while maintaining high aerodynamic efficiency over extended missions.

Equipped with an AI-enabled EO/IR optical payload, it provides real-time day-and-night imaging for border surveillance, area monitoring, and search and rescue operations. The platform serves as a dual-use system, equally suited for civilian and defence applications including infrastructure inspection, disaster response, and persistent wide-area observation. It can also function as an airborne command node, coordinating with other aerial assets including loitering munitions. Hybrid electric-gasoline propulsion enables airborne endurance exceeding 8 hours and mission coverage up to 100 km.

Responsibilities covered the full design cycle from conceptual sizing and detailed CAD modelling to structural and aerodynamic evaluation, design for manufacturing, avionics integration, and path and mission planning.

The platform was demonstrated at a university guidance session for other students.

Academic project 2024 · Structural Design

Heavy-Duty Trailer — Structural Design & FEA Validation

Designed and validated a heavy-duty trailer for a 1000 kg payload with a target factor of safety exceeding 2.0, balancing structural robustness with cost-efficiency and manufacturability. The chassis uses C-section carbon steel beams with a reinforced load grid, precision hinge mounts, and leaf spring fixtures engineered for long-term reliability under repeated loading.

FEA analysis confirmed a chassis FOS of 2.7 with a natural frequency range of 403–3276 Hz, comfortably clear of operational excitation frequencies. Axle verification achieved FOS 2.0 with a 60.3 Hz natural frequency. Leaf springs and M10 bolts were independently validated under full load conditions. The final design came in at 256.7 kg with an estimated manufacturing cost of Rs. 124k — optimised for strength, vibration safety, and direct manufacturing readiness.

Academic project 2024 · Vehicle Design & FEA

Dune Buggy — Space Frame Design & Structural Analysis

Designed and analysed a tubular steel space-frame dune buggy optimised for off-road performance and structural safety. The chassis integrates a full roll cage, 4-wheel independent suspension with coilover dampers, and a dedicated battery and electronics bay — reflecting a hybrid or electric drivetrain architecture. The top-down layout was optimised for balanced weight distribution and suspension geometry, with mounting points for all major drivetrain and chassis components.

Structural validation was performed through FEA using SolidWorks Simulation. Resultant displacement analysis showed a maximum deformation of 0.12 mm under load, while equivalent strain remained within acceptable limits (max 6.921e-05), confirming the frame's rigidity and structural integrity across static loading conditions. The results validate the space frame geometry for the intended off-road use case.

Design project 2026 · Wing Systems

UAV Wing Assembly — Slat, Flap & Aileron Integration

Designed a fully integrated UAV wing assembly incorporating three independently actuated control surfaces: a leading-edge slat for high-lift augmentation, a trailing-edge flap for camber and lift enhancement, and an aileron for roll control. All surfaces are servo-actuated via SG90 micro servos connected through pushrod linkage mechanisms, providing precise, low-friction control authority across the full deflection range.

The wing structure is built around a carbon spar as the primary load-carrying member, with mixed-material construction for the remaining components. Servo deflection ranges are 25–45° for the slat and 20–30° for the flap and aileron. The design was documented to full engineering drawing standard, including an exploded assembly drawing with BOM, section views (A-A, B-B), and detail views of all servo mounting and pushrod locking interfaces.
